# ROLLING DICE

To play, you need dice with four, six, eight, ten, twelve and twenty sides, commonly referred to as d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, d20. 

![[assets/Screenshot 2026-08-30 at 10.04.19 PM.png]]

When facing uncertainty, Outcasts roll dice to find an answer. Higher is always better, both for the size of the dice and the result of their rolls. The goal is to meet or exceed a number set by the Host, called the Difficulty (DIFF).

You always roll a base d12, accompanied by a die relevant to the skill or reaction currently used. It varies between d0 (nothing), d4, d6, d8, d10 and d12. 

**Roll those two dice together.**
![[assets/Screenshot 2026-08-30 at 10.04.31 PM.png]]

Take the highest rolled value and add the relevant bonus. This is your **Effort.** If it meets or exceeds the Difficulty (Diff.), your attempt succeeds.
![[assets/Screenshot 2026-08-30 at 10.04.51 PM.png]]

Take the lower rolled value without adding any bonuses. This is your **Impact.** Compare it to the chart below to determine _how well_ you succeed.

If you did not roll a second die (such as when you are unskilled), your Impact roll is always a 1. 
![[assets/Screenshot 2026-08-30 at 10.05.13 PM.png]]
The degrees of Impact are mostly broad narrative categories meant to help the Host figure out what to narrate. For some actions, such as attacking and casting a spell, the degrees of Impact have specific meanings or numerical values defined by the rules.

# Advantage / Disadvantage

You will sometimes be called to roll with Adv‹?› (short for advantage) or Disadv‹?› (short for disadvantage). This simply means you reroll the higher (Adv) or lower (Disadv) result as many time as the specified ‹number› and keep the new highest or lowest result, accordingly.  

Adv‹?› and Disadv‹?› of the same number cancel each other. Two sources of Adv‹1› or Disadv‹1› do not make an Adv‹2› or Disadv‹2›.

# Opposed Rolls

Sometimes, a creature's roll is used to set the difficulty of another creature’s roll. In such cases, use the rolls’ Impact to break ties. If both Impacts are the same, the defender wins.

# Skill Rolls

Anytime you ask _could I do_\_\_\_ ?\_ and the answer is uncertain, it is found using Skill rolls. When making a Skill roll (often simply called _rolling a skill_), the Host will always specify the Aspect of the roll. They might ask for _Traversal with Flesh_ or _Negotiation with Heart or Spark_. When the skill roll is made against another creature, it sets the difficulty. Therefore, in a given situation, your performance might be enough to best one creature but not another. 

In most cases, there are multiple ways to approach a problem, and you should think about which to choose. Be specific rather than vague. Instead of declaring ”_I want to avoid the guards_”, opt for "_I want to create a diversion_" or "_I want to stealthily get past the guards_", or even “_I want to bribe the guards to let me pass_”. Each of these will take your story in different and more interesting directions

# Reactions

Reaction rolls happen when you act with instinct or when your conscious skills fail you. You will Brace, Defy, Dodge and Handle your way out of harm, each reaction having its own range of purposes. The Host states the available options and the difficulties. 

**The difficulty of a Reaction roll should always be announced before the roll is made.** 

![[assets/Screenshot 2026-08-30 at 10.09.24 PM.png]]

**Brace**
Holding your ground as you block a heavy blow, not loosening your grip on a rope as you suffer incredible pain, keeping mobile under the effect of a numbing poison. Brace is the only way to defend against attacks made by Rushing enemies. 

**Defy**
Resisting the effects of a a spell, fighting back against the Fog's influence, keeping your composure when confronted to your greatest fear. Intangible spells must be Defied, but tangible spells can also sometimes be Dodged or Braced against.

**Dodge**
Deftly avoiding a weapon's swing, diving outside of a blast radius, squeezing yourself between two stakes as you fall in a pit trap. Dodge requires free space around you to use.

**Handle**
Handle relies on your cool-headedness and finesse to improvise solutions. It is to interrupt a triggering trap,  to catch an object in mid-air, to put everything where it was mere seconds before someone walks in.

> \[!Example in Play]
> August is playing for the first time. His character Zap, an impish arcanist, is trying to haggle with a merchant, but it’s proving difficult. He has been found out as an Outcast, and the merchant wants nothing to do with him. August looks at Zap’s skills for an idea. He is far better at Business(d6) than Empathy(d0), so he decides to play hardball. 
>
> _“I want to threathen him softly, suggesting the faster he agrees to sell, the faster I’ll be out of sight. I’m content to stay here all day and ruin his business, you know?”_
>
> The Host declares this a **cunning** play and asks if August has the Cunning Tag. August does. The Host requests a _Business roll with Heart_ and sets the difficulty to 10 (Diff‹10› for short), but lowers it to Diff‹8› on account of Zap's **cunning**.
>
> August rolls the Base d12 and his Business d6 together.  He gets a 4 on the d12 and a 5 on the d6. He takes the highest number rolled (5) and adds his Heart (3). 
>
> With a total of 8, he barely meets the Difficulty and succeeds. He then looks at the other number (4) and compares it to the Impact table. got a Minor Impact because he rolled between 1 and 3. 
>
> Each Success means one good thing. With his Major Success (two good things), Zap convinced the merchant to do business, and made him so mad he miscounted the change and gave back too much.
