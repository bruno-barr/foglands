Trials are your movie scenes, moments where so much happens that there is a need to slow down. The game switches to a Trial when time is of the essence, when your life is at risk, or when you attempt something so interesting or complex that it deserves scrutiny. Classic examples include exploring mysterious caves, escaping deadly pursuit, negociating a crucial deal or fighting monsters.

## Freeform vs Strict

The Trial is the most flexible game mode of the three, because it is a Scene that happens _in real time_. It is, for lack of a better term, the normal (and only) playing experience of most tabletop RPGs. Characters interact with the world around them. They discuss, make decisions, move and act together towards their immediate goal.

```
Unlike Journeys or Interludes, Trials don't require you to always adhere to the now familiar Rounds and Turns. Indeed, the Host should let players move about the map in a freeform manner, until something slows the story down to seconds, such as combat, chases, triggered traps, etc. Using freeform play as much as possible keeps Trials dynamic, saves an incredible amount of time and makes going into Turn orders feel important.
```
