# Initiative

When violence erupts, the Outcasts and the Host roll dice, and the side with the highest total acts first. In case of a tie, Players act first. All Players rolls and accrue a Fog die of the size they desire, and pool the results together. The Host rolls 1d12 + (1 per Outcast). **Players always have the option to forsake the roll.**

# Surprise

If one side is unaware of the danger as the game shifts to a Trial,  they become **surprised**, meaning they act last and cannot accrue Fog in the first round of combat.

# Leaving Combat

Combat ends when all Outcasts or their foes have died, fled or become unable or unwilling to fight. 

# What is a Turn?

A Turn is a way to give ownership of a specific moment in time. The creatures in a Scene take their Turn one side after another to execute their actions. Whether it takes 3 seconds or 10 minutes, a Turn in the spotlight is about doing a meaningful thing fitting the time scale.

_You take two actions to move in and search a room. The enemies do the same to set an ambush on the other side of the door. Time compresses to seconds and swings of swords._

# Rounds

In Trials, Rounds represent how long it takes for all creatures to have their Turn in the spotlight once. They serve as a unit of measurement for the length of abilities, effects and events of all kinds. Rounds always unfold in this order:

**Round Start\
****\_+ Upkeep +\
\_****&#x54;urns**\
**Round End**

## Round Start

All creatures can Move‹8› and decide whether or not they are **rushing** (sacrificing 1 of their 2 actions to act _in this phase._)

All rushing creatures act at the same time. When two rushing creatures attack each other, they make opposed attack rolls, and only the winner gets to act. Otherwise, attacks made against other rushing creatures that aren’t attacking back automatically hit.

```
Rushing is useful when one is confident in their skill rolls, when trying to prevent something from occurring during Upkeep, or when trying to act first despite having lost the initiative. contest.
```

## Upkeep

This phase is used to resolve recurring effects, hazards and timed events. This is where Outcasts accrue the recurring FOG to sustain spells and roll said spells’ effects, where you take your recurring damage from poison, where the water level increases and where reinforcements flood the scene.

## Turns

**Perform 2 actions**
This is where most creatures act. The side that holds the Initiative acts first, with all of its creatures doing so at once. Conflicting effects are resolved in the order that produces the most favorable outcome for that side. 

## Round End

This phase is where effects entering in their last round of duration run out.

# Attacking

**Attacking**

An attack is nothing more than a skill roll with restrictions.

**I.** Choose a target to attack and a method (Weapon, Spell, Feat, etc) and its associated skill. 

**II.** Make the skill roll as usual.

**III.** Each degree of Impact corresponds to a specific amount of damage or effect (as shown in your attack’s description).

**Targeted Strike**

When attacking single creatures (as opposed to Hordes), you can target a specific body part by rolling against Diff +1 (or Diff +2 for the head.) 

**Defending**

Your Defence, expressed as a number, represents your ability to effortlessly block and parry incoming harm. When the total damage suffered within a round is higher than your Defence, you are overwhelmed and suffer a Wound (see Damage section below).

When you are targeted by damage or an effect, you can trust in your Defence, or spend 1 Stamina to make a Reaction roll against the attacker. This reaction roll applies to all attacks the creature makes against you this round. While most attacks can be Braced or Dodged, some require a specific Reaction.

Reaction rolls function identically to Skill rolls. 

**I.** Roll a d12 and the Reaction die together.

**II.** Apply the Reaction’s bonus to the highest result to beat the Diff.

**III.** Take the lower result without any bonuses and compare to the correct Reaction table below.

## Brace  

Bracing always means getting hit, but mitigating some, most or even all damage. Great when the incoming damage is too great for you to risk dodging. Wearing a shield raises your Defence and makes your first Brace roll free each round.

## Defy

In combat, you might be called to Defy the mental intrusions of fog spawns, the effect of some spells, or even your own fear in the face of unimaginable horrors.

## Dodge

When you Dodge a threat, the Impact of your roll determines how far you can move. Moving out of the attack's path means avoiding the damage or effect. This movement must be used immediately. Other sources of movement cannot be used here
